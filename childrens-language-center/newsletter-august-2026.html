Back-to-School Communication Tips

Simple ways to support communication, routines, confidence, and connection as children head back to school.


← Back to Newsletter
From Children's Language Center

Back-to-school season brings new teachers, new routines, new environments, and lots of opportunities for communication. For some children, that can feel exciting. For others, it can feel like a lot all at once.

1. Practice the school-day routine before the first day

Talk through the morning routine using short, predictable language. You can name each step - getting dressed, eating breakfast, packing the backpack, riding to school, and saying goodbye.

Try this at home: Use simple sequence words like “first,” “next,” and “last.” For example: “First shoes, next backpack, then school.”

2. Give your child words for asking for help

Children may know what they need but still have trouble finding the words in the moment. Practicing a few useful phrases ahead of time can make school feel more manageable.

Depending on your child's communication level, practice phrases such as “Help me,” “I need a break,” “Can you show me?” or “I don't understand.” Gestures, pictures, and other communication supports count too.

3. Ask specific questions after school

Instead of asking “How was school?” try questions that give your child something more concrete to think about.

Conversation starters: “What made you smile today?”
“Who did you sit next to?”
“What did you play?”
“What was your favorite part of lunch?”

4. Use books to talk about school feelings

Books about school can help children name feelings, predict what might happen, and practice language around new experiences. Pause while reading and give your child time to comment, point, or answer.

5. Keep communication pressure low

Starting school is a big transition. Children may need more processing time or may communicate differently when tired or overwhelmed. Focus on connection first, and give them room to respond in the way that works best for them.
